> _The personal reflection that closes your final project._

## Friction points

- **The data disagreed with my hook (M3).** I started from the moment of misery: a re-route takes 8–15 min to reach the driver. The data check showed the biggest measured gap was somewhere else: compliance, 14.6 min against a 3.0 benchmark. The hard part was choosing between keeping a clean story and following the data. I kept both by reframing the problem as one "assignment loop," with the re-route and compliance in the same flow. That made every later step harder.
- **Fitting it into 2 engineers and 4 weeks (M4).** B1 (compliance) was the largest gap, but it was too big to build next to B6, and I couldn't scope it down. So I had to accept that the pilot alone couldn't prove the whole hypothesis.
- **The "one change" rule (M5).** A clean A/B test meant pulling B5 out of my own pilot and testing B6 alone. Then the honest prediction was "pivot," not "ship." It's uncomfortable to design a test you expect to fall short of your own threshold.

## Key learnings

- **AI follows my framing; it doesn't make it.** In M1, the mind map only became useful once I gave it a persona, a job to be done and an effort-vs-impact frame. In M2, the AI summary sounded complete, but it was written mostly from the driver's side and reduced the dispatcher's double entry to a few scattered bullets. It also gave severity labels to interview findings that had no severity. I now read AI output the way I'd review a junior analyst's work: check the evidence, not the tone.
- **Thin evidence is a hypothesis, not a fact.** Only 2 of 12 research notes came from dispatchers. Saying so in the deck made the story stronger, not weaker.
- **Cutting is the real roadmap work.** Saying no to B8 (Sales-driven) and B2 (a risk to the reporting guardrail) did more for focus than any feature I added.
- **A launch is behaviour change.** In M6, the plan was less about channels than about getting two groups to drop a habit: coordinators leave WhatsApp, and drivers allow push.

## Aha! moment

The value was never "fewer clicks." It was "no phone call." Coordinators don't leave RouteLogic because it takes three taps. They leave because the change doesn't reach the driver, so they have to do it again somewhere else. Once I saw that the competitor to beat was the WhatsApp group, not another app, every decision got simpler: the hook, the PRD's ≤ 5 s requirement, the primary metric and the GTM message.
