01 · Module 1 · Problem Hook & Value Proposition

Problem hook

We must stop our largest accounts from switching to leaner competitors by making a live re-route reach the driver in seconds, so dispatchers no longer run the day from a WhatsApp group.

RouteLogic will lose its largest enterprise account to a leaner competitor, and others will follow. The people who run daily operations will move to faster tools. RouteLogic will be left as an expensive back-office system that nobody uses in real time. We will no longer be "the fastest tool on the market," just the most complicated one.

When a delivery goes wrong mid-route, the dispatcher reassigns it in RouteLogic, but the driver doesn't see the change for 8–15 minutes and gets no notification. So the dispatcher sends it again in a WhatsApp group or calls the driver, and stops trusting the board. In one dispatcher's words: "We keep a WhatsApp group as the real system." Meanwhile, an enterprise account is evaluating a leaner competitor that "just does routing well."

UXR-02 · UXR-04 · BUG-2044

For regional fleet dispatchers and the drivers they coordinate, we will deliver a focused Velocity workspace where a re-route reaches the driver in seconds and is confirmed in one tap, with the enterprise features kept one layer away rather than removed, because an enterprise account is already evaluating a leaner competitor and renewal is at risk now.

Based on dispatchers running the day from a WhatsApp group (UXR-02) and only 18% of coordinators finishing their daily workflow in RouteLogic, I believe that a focused Velocity workspace that closes the assignment loop (the re-route reaches the driver in seconds and compliance is logged in the same flow) will move coordinators back into RouteLogic and reduce complexity-driven churn, as measured by a 2× increase in in-tool workflow completion (18% → 36%). 07 · Module 6 · Individual Insights

What I learned

Aha moment

The value was never "fewer clicks." It was "no phone call."

Coordinators don't leave RouteLogic because it takes three taps. They leave because the change doesn't reach the driver, so they have to do it again somewhere else. Once I saw that the competitor to beat was the WhatsApp group, not another app, every decision got simpler: the hook, the PRD's ≤ 5 s requirement, the primary metric and the GTM message.

Friction points

The data disagreed with my hook (M3).

Show all ↓

I started from the moment of misery: a re-route takes 8–15 min to reach the driver. The data check showed the biggest measured gap was somewhere else: compliance, 14.6 min against a 3.0 benchmark. The hard part was choosing between keeping a clean story and following the data. I kept both by reframing the problem as one "assignment loop," with the re-route and compliance in the same flow. That made every later step harder.

Fitting it into 2 engineers and 4 weeks (M4). B1 (compliance) was the largest gap, but it was too big to build next to B6, and I couldn't scope it down. So I had to accept that the pilot alone couldn't prove the whole hypothesis.

The "one change" rule (M5). A clean A/B test meant pulling B5 out of my own pilot and testing B6 alone. Then the honest prediction was "pivot," not "ship." It's uncomfortable to design a test you expect to fall short of your own threshold.

Key learnings

AI follows my framing; it doesn't make it.

Show all ↓

In M1, the mind map only became useful once I gave it a persona, a job to be done and an effort-vs-impact frame. In M2, the AI summary sounded complete, but it was written mostly from the driver's side and reduced the dispatcher's double entry to a few scattered bullets. It also gave severity labels to interview findings that had no severity. I now read AI output the way I'd review a junior analyst's work: check the evidence, not the tone.

Thin evidence is a hypothesis, not a fact. Only 2 of 12 research notes came from dispatchers. Saying so in the deck made the story stronger, not weaker.

Cutting is the real roadmap work. Saying no to B8 (Sales-driven) and B2 (a risk to the reporting guardrail) did more for focus than any feature I added.

A launch is behaviour change. In M6, the plan was less about channels than about getting two groups to drop a habit: coordinators leave WhatsApp, and drivers allow push.
