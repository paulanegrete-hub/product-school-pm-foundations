# Your Final Project — Final Project Brief

*Product Management Foundations · Final Project*

**Take one product from problem to launch.** Across six modules you build six deliverables inside one repo, then turn them into a presentation in Module 6 and submit both.

Individual · Built in class · Repo + presentation · Due 7 days after your cohort

## Choose your product

Commit to one of the two scenarios below, or bring an instructor-approved problem from your own organization. You carry your choice through every module.

### StreamLine (Scenario · B2C)

Imagine you are a Product Manager at StreamLine, a global streaming platform. For over a decade the company has prioritized aggressive content acquisition to build the largest library in the market. Today StreamLine hosts over 15,000 titles, from major blockbusters to niche documentaries, and that "more is better" strategy has been its primary competitive advantage.

The market is shifting. StreamLine still leads on total volume, but quarterly data shows engagement plateauing and subscriber churn rising. Smaller, specialized services focused on high-quality, curated experiences are gaining ground, and there is growing concern that by trying to serve everyone, StreamLine is alienating the loyal, high-value viewers who care most about discovery.

**Your challenge.** The library keeps growing while StreamLine's authority as a place for discovery declines. You have to decide whether a more curated, human-centered approach can close the engagement gap without giving up the mass-market scale the business was built on, and whether a high-end, niche experience can live inside a platform designed for everyone.

**Your goal:** lead the development and potential launch of StreamLine Spotlight, a premium space within the app dedicated to curated cinema, using existing user research to define how it should work and win back the viewers StreamLine has been losing.

### RouteLogic (Scenario · B2B)

Imagine you are a Product Manager at RouteLogic, a logistics platform used by regional delivery fleets. Its success was built on handling immense complexity, an all-in-one suite covering everything from global shipping compliance to executive financial reporting. To the C-suite, RouteLogic is the most powerful operating system a modern logistics business could run.

That expanding scope has opened a gap between administrative power and frontline usefulness. The software is technically superior, but internal data shows a sharp decline in usage among the people responsible for real-time fleet coordination. Your largest enterprise account is trialing a leaner competitor, calling RouteLogic an administrative burden that slows their most time-sensitive work, and the board worries the product's complexity is now hindering the very operations it was meant to speed up.

**Your challenge.** After years of saying yes to everything, RouteLogic has become a collection of specialized features that only a small fraction of customers use, and for the 95% who rely on it daily those extra layers have turned a simple job into a struggle. You have to work out how to strip the distractions and restore the core value while keeping the high-end enterprise capabilities available so they stay out of the everyday user's way.

**Your goal:** lead the strategy for RouteLogic Velocity, an initiative to reclaim the product's operational simplicity, auditing the current experience with existing user research to decide which features are essential and which are legacy noise, so the platform proves it can still be the fastest tool on the market.

**Bring your own.** Prefer a real problem from your own organization? You can, with your instructor's approval.

## Six deliverables, one per module

Each one is built in that module's hands-on lab and committed to your repo. Together they are your final project.

1. **Problem Hook & Value Prop** — The problem statement and the hypothesis that grows out of it.
2. **Competitive Intel & Journey** — Your workaround analysis and a high-level journey map.
3. **Roadmap, PRD & Prototype** — The roadmap, PRD, and wireframes or prototype of your solution.
4. **Experimentation Plan** — How you'd validate the hypothesis before you build for real.
5. **GTM & Success Dashboard** — Your go-to-market summary and the KPIs you'd watch.
6. **Individual Insights** — Your reflection: the friction, the learning, the aha moment.

## One repo holds it all, then becomes your presentation

Your repo is where every lab's work lives. You create it once from the course template, drop in one artifact per module, and in Module 6 the Final Project Deliverables Builder pulls those artifacts into a single HTML presentation you download and submit with your repo link. The repo is the body of work; the presentation is how you tell it.

## Submit two things

## Built with you, in class

It's an **individual** project, so there's no coordinating with peers between sessions. Every module gives you dedicated, hands-on time to build that module's artifact, and Module 6 gives you time to turn them into your presentation with the Final Project Deliverables Builder.

If you don't finish an activity in the session, or you want to revisit, redo, or sharpen your work, plan to carve out a little of your own time.

## Questions, answered

**When is it due, and where do I submit?** Submit your repo URL and your presentation on the Learning Platform within 7 days of your cohort ending. You can still submit after that and earn your certification, but you will not receive written feedback from your instructor.

**Should my repo be public or private?** Start private. Keep it private and share access with your instructor for grading, and with peers by direct link if you want their feedback. You can make it public later if you prefer, just know that public repos are searchable and expose your work and the course content. GitHub's docs show [how to change a repo's visibility](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/managing-repository-settings/setting-repository-visibility).

**What if I miss a session or fall behind?** Every artifact stands on its own, so you can finish it on your own time and commit it when it is ready. If you miss a live session, you can watch the recording from the link in the Learning Platform.

**Do I have to present live?** Your instructor will ask for volunteers first; if nobody volunteers, a participant may be called at random. Either way, it's a chance to get live feedback on your work, not a polish check.

**Can I use my own product instead of StreamLine or RouteLogic?** Yes, bring a real problem from your own organization, as long as your instructor approves it before Module 1.
