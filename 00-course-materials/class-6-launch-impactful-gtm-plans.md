## 1 · What makes an impactful GTM plan?

### Four launches, four lessons

Every PM dreams of launching the next iPhone. Most launches are forgotten within a week; others become case studies in what not to do. The difference is rarely the product, it's the plan behind it.

- **📱 Apple iPhone (success)**: the announcement *was* the launch. Apple created demand six months before the product shipped by controlling the narrative, the moment, and the message. **Lesson:** a Big Bang launch done right doesn't just introduce a product, it defines a category. The launch moment doesn't have to coincide with the ship date.

- **💬 ChatGPT (success)**: no paid media, no formal GTM, no launch event. 1M users in 5 days, 100M in two months, entirely through organic sharing. **Lesson:** when product-market fit is real, the product launches itself. GTM amplifies PMF, it doesn't create it.

- **🥤 New Coke (flop)**: blind taste tests said people preferred the new formula, so they launched with confidence. The research missed that Coke was a cultural institution, not just a taste. Reversed in 79 days. **Lesson:** quantitative data tells you what users *do*, not what they *feel*. Qualitative research exists for exactly this.

- **👓 Google Glass (flop)**: impressive technology, no clear everyday use case, $1,500 price, privacy backlash. The GTM wasn't the problem; the product-market fit was. **Lesson:** no launch tier, channel plan, or messaging can compensate for a product the market isn't ready for.

**The through line: GTM is a multiplier, not a miracle worker. It amplifies what's already there.**

### A GTM plan is a PM output, not a marketing deliverable

A product can be technically perfect and still fail at launch. The GTM plan is the PM's strategic output. It answers three questions every launch must resolve:

1. **Who needs to know about this?**: not just customers, but internal teams, partners, and support.

2. **How do people find out?**: which channels, via which assets, in which sequence.

3. **How do you know it worked?**: the metrics that show the launch achieved its goal, not just that the product shipped.

It's a **parallel workstream** that starts the moment a feature moves from discovery into build. In smaller teams the PM owns it; in larger orgs a PMM may lead, either way, understanding it is non-negotiable for every PM.

**The 7-part launch checklist:** goal & audience · launch tier · channel plan · enablement & assets · budget & resources · timeline · success metrics.

### Setting your goal (the goal determines the audience)

- **Awareness**: make the market aware the product exists and how it's perceived. Right for new markets, new audiences, or repositioning. Audience is external. *PM action: define the narrative; ensure the marketing message matches product reality.*

- **Engagement**: drive interaction among existing users. If a feature ships, users won't automatically find it. Audience lives inside your base. *PM action: monitor activation; find the friction blocking the aha moment.*

- **Conversion**: connect the launch to a business outcome. Needs the most precise targeting. Audience is people ready to move to the next stage. *PM action: brief sales on the target customer; set trial-to-paid benchmarks upfront.*

### Determining your launch tier

Not every launch deserves the same investment. Over-investing in a small release is as costly as under-investing in a major one. Size with **t-shirt sizing**:

- **XL**: full GTM (press, events, demos, broad paid media). *e.g. Apple's iPhone events, Notion's public launch.*

- **L**: multi-channel, targeted scope. *e.g. Slack introducing huddles.*

- **M**: focused email, in-app messaging, a blog post.

- **S**: minimal or no outreach; the changelog updates and the right users find it.

And ask: **is this a launch or a release?** A release is technically ready for use; a launch activates go-to-market. They don't have to coincide, never let a formal launch delay shipping something valuable.

### Three channels every launch needs

- **Owned** (all tiers), email, in-app messages, push, social handles. Cost nothing to activate; only reach people already in your ecosystem.

- **Earned** (L/XL), press, reviews, word of mouth, social mentions. The most credible distribution, others talking about you, and the hardest to control. Create the conditions: a well-timed launch, a compelling story, a product worth talking about.

- **Paid** (L/XL), paid search, paid social, influencers. Solve the reach problem owned channels can't. The PM decision isn't *which* paid channel but *whether the tier justifies the budget.*

**The channel follows the audience.** The most common mistake is choosing channels before defining the audience, if your goal is to re-engage lapsed users, in-app messages will never reach them.

### Enablement for internal stakeholders

A product can ship perfectly and still land badly if the people closest to customers don't know what to say. Sales, CS, and Support are who customers contact first.

- **Enablement brief** (minimum): product description ("type of product + main benefit"), key features written with action verbs, common objections, what's changing and what's not.

- **Assets by tier:** all tiers → one-pagers, demos, website copy; sales-led → slides, demo environments, customer stories; XL → webinars, launch videos, interactive tours.

- **Launch tracker**: one living source of truth: action items + owners, risks to monitor, key decisions log. Without it, launches rely on memory and Slack threads.

## 2 · A PM's practical toolkit for launch

### Resources & budget planning

The most common GTM failure isn't a bad strategy, it's a good strategy with no one accountable for executing it.

- **Ownership, who owns what?** Every task needs a *named individual*, not a team. Teams don't send emails; individuals do. The later ownership is assigned, the less time that person has to do the work well.

- **Budgeting, what costs extra?** Media spend, influencer fees, and asset production must be requested *before* the plan is finalised. A budget ask after the campaign is live won't be approved in time.

- **Assets vs. resources, what needs building?** Every asset needs a builder and the skill/tool to build it. A launch video with no producer is an asset that won't get made. Surface gaps now, not in launch week.

### The AI toolkit for launches

The biggest GTM bottleneck isn't knowing what to do, it's the bandwidth to do it. AI removes manual steps so a PM can run a coordinated launch without a full team.

1. **Content & assets** (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini), prototype emails, blog posts, one-pagers; shift the conversation with marketing from "can you write this" to "can you refine this."

2. **Design & visuals** (Canva AI, Gamma), prototype ideas early so the brief you hand a designer is already directionally right.

3. **Automation agents** (n8n, Zapier, Claude), string together GTM workflows; trigger launch sequences automatically. The biggest real time savings.

4. **Analytics & signals** (GA4, Mixpanel, Amplitude), surface adoption patterns and flag anomalies fast enough to act while the launch is still live.

**AI doesn't replace your marketing team, it removes the bottleneck of not having one.**

### What AI cannot replace

- **Genuine market intuition**: AI synthesises what's known; it can't surface insight from conversations that haven't happened. Real customer contact is non-negotiable.

- **Contrarian strategic bets**: the best positioning is usually the one AI doesn't suggest, because it's trained on what everyone else does. Differentiation is human judgment.

- **Relationships that build trust**: community, executive alignment, sales relationships depend on authenticity. AI in these moments is detected instantly and erodes trust.

**Use AI to execute your thinking, not to replace it. If you can't critique the output, you don't understand the domain well enough to use the tool responsibly.**

### The three launch phases

Most teams treat launch day as the finish line. The best treat it as the *midpoint* of a three-phase plan. The AI toolkit maps directly: automation in Phase 1, content in Phase 2, analytics in Phase 3.

- **Phase 1 · Beta / early access**: learn before scaling. Invite feedback, pressure-test messaging, build advocates. *(Notion spent years in closed beta.)* Track: retention curve + feature adoption.

- **Phase 2 · Launch moment**: the broadest awareness moment; all internal teams live. Over-invested by most teams at the expense of phases 1 and 3, it only works if the groundwork was laid. Track: activation rate among early users.

- **Phase 3 · Post-launch adoption**: turn the spike into a trend. Sustain momentum, deepen adoption, feed learnings back into the roadmap. Track: adoption velocity + error rate.

**A launch is a coordinated sequence of decisions that starts the moment a feature moves into build and ends when the data tells you what comes next.**

### Post-launch metrics

A launch without measurement is just an event. Measure against the *goal you set*, an awareness campaign judged purely on conversion will always misread the result.

| Goal | Track | Bad signal → diagnosis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Awareness** | Reach, new signups, landing-page visits | High reach, zero signups → **message-market mismatch** (fix messaging, not channel) |
| **Engagement** | Feature adoption rate, DAU, time to first key action | Signups, low TFA → **onboarding failure** (fix activation, not acquisition) |
| **Conversion** | Conversion rate, trial-to-paid, retention | Trials, no upgrades → **pricing / value-prop problem** (fix positioning, not trial volume) |

Three levels of signal: **feature** (is it used?), **product** (is it changing behaviour?), **business** (did it move the needle?). The **AARRR framework from Module 3** maps directly, the metrics don't just measure the launch, they tell you what to build next.

### What's next? Post-launch decisions

What users do after launch is the most honest signal a PM will ever get.

- **Double-down**: sustained signal (a trend over weeks, not a first-week spike) → expand the rollout and invest.

- **Iterate**: mixed signals → change *one* variable (message, channel, or audience) so the next read is clean.

- **Pivot**: unexpected results → adjust positioning, audience, or product. The most underutilised move: ask *why* users behave differently than planned.

- **Deprioritize**: weak signals → log learnings and redirect resources. Not failure, it protects the roadmap.

Unlike experiment outcomes (a controlled test result), post-launch iteration reads **real market behaviour over time.**

## Key takeaways

1. **A GTM plan is the PM's strategic output, not a marketing handoff.** Build it in parallel with the product and your launch lands with intent.

2. **Goal → audience, tier → effort, channels → reach, in sequence.** This is what makes a GTM plan coherent and executable.

3. **Internal enablement is as important as external execution.** Brief stakeholders before anything goes live so customers are supported from day one.

4. **Post-launch data is only valuable if it drives a decision.** Treat the launch as the start of a learning cycle and your products compound in value with every iteration.

## Glossary

# Module 6 · Glossary: Write the Launch Plan and How You Will Know

Quick reference for the terms used in Module 6. Keep this open during the lab.

## Core concepts

- **GTM (go-to-market) plan**: the PM's strategic output for a launch. It answers three questions: *who needs to know about this, how do they find out, and how do we know it worked?* It runs in parallel with the build, not after it.

- **The 7-part launch checklist**: goal & audience → launch tier → channel plan → enablement & assets → budget & resources → timeline → success metrics.

- **Launch vs. release**: a **release** is when the product is technically ready for customer use; a **launch** is when you activate go-to-market efforts around it. They don't need to coincide, and some capabilities can simply be released without a launch.

- **GTM as a multiplier**: go-to-market amplifies a product that already has product-market fit; it cannot create fit that isn't there.

## Goal & audience

- **Awareness goal**: make the market aware the product exists and how it should be perceived. Audience is usually external (new prospects, adjacent users, new segments).

- **Engagement goal**: drive meaningful interaction among existing users (adoption, feedback, loyalty). Audience lives inside your current user base.

- **Conversion goal**: connect the launch to a measurable business outcome (acquire, upsell, retain). Audience is precise, people ready to move to the next stage.

- **The goal determines the audience**: not the other way around. A conversion goal without a defined segment is wishful thinking.

## Launch tier (t-shirt sizing)

- **S, Minimal**: no proactive outreach; the feature ships and the right users find it.

- **M, Targeted**: limited channels, focused comms (e.g. email + in-app + a blog post).

- **L, Multi-channel**: select tactics with targeted reach; a solid GTM plan across several channels.

- **XL, Full GTM**: press, events, demos, broad paid media; reserved for the most significant launches.

## Channels

- **Owned**: channels a company fully controls (email, in-app messages, push, social handles). In almost every plan; only reach people already in your ecosystem.

- **Earned**: channels you don't control but can optimise for (press, reviews, word of mouth, social mentions). The most credible, others talking about you, and the hardest to manufacture.

- **Paid**: channels that require budget to reach beyond your base (paid search, paid social, influencer marketing). Used when owned channels alone can't hit the goal.

- **The channel follows the audience**: match each channel to where your audience already is, not the other way around.

## Enablement

- **Enablement brief**: the document that ensures every internal stakeholder knows what's launching and how it works. At minimum: product description, key features, common objections, what's changing.

- **Enablement assets**: the tangible deliverables to build (one-pagers and demos for all tiers; sales slides and customer stories for sales-led; webinars and product tours for XL).

- **Launch tracker**: the living source of truth: action items + owners, risks to monitor, and a key-decisions log.

## Execution

- **Ownership**: every GTM task needs a *named individual*, not a team. A task owned by everyone is owned by no one.

- **Budget ask**: anything that costs money (media spend, influencer fees, asset production) must be requested *before* the plan is finalised.

- **Asset vs. resource gap**: every asset needs someone to build it and the skill/tool to build it with; a launch video with no producer won't get made.

- **AI launch toolkit**: content & assets (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini), design & visuals (Canva AI, Gamma), automation agents (n8n, Zapier), analytics & signals (GA4, Mixpanel, Amplitude). AI removes the bottleneck of not having a marketing team, it doesn't replace one.

- **What AI can't replace**: genuine market intuition, contrarian strategic bets, and relationships that build trust.

## The three launch phases

- **Phase 1 · Beta / early access**: invite feedback, pressure-test messaging, build advocates. Track: retention curve + feature adoption.

- **Phase 2 · Launch moment**: create the broadest awareness moment; all internal teams live. Track: activation rate among early users.

- **Phase 3 · Post-launch adoption**: convert awareness into long-term behaviour. Track: adoption velocity + error rate.

## Post-launch metrics & decisions

- **Match metrics to the goal**: awareness (reach, signups, landing-page visits), engagement (feature adoption, DAU, time to first key action), conversion (conversion rate, trial-to-paid, retention).

- **Bad signal**: a diagnostic pattern: high reach + zero signups = message-market mismatch; signups + low time-to-first-action = onboarding failure; trials + no upgrades = pricing/value-prop problem.

- **Time to first key action (TFA)**: how quickly a new user reaches their first meaningful moment with the feature.

- **The four post-launch decisions**: **double-down** (sustained signal confirms it's working), **iterate** (mixed signals; change one variable), **pivot** (unexpected results; adjust positioning/audience/product), **deprioritize** (weak signals; log learnings and redirect resources).

- **Post-launch iteration vs. experiment result**: an experiment outcome is a controlled test; post-launch iteration reads *real market behaviour over time*.
